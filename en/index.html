About the village

A living heritage

Duong Lam lies in the old Xu Doai region at the foot of Ba Vi mountain, and people have lived here for more than a thousand years. In 2006 it became the first village in Vietnam to be listed as a national heritage site – not a single temple or pagoda, but a whole village: houses, lanes, wells, communal houses and the way of life of its people. Unlike a museum, people still live in these homes.

From our family’s old house in Mong Phu village, we invite you to discover Duong Lam through its stories, home-cooked meals and everyday local life.

The colour of laterite

Walls, village gates and well rims are built from laterite dug from the village’s own soil – reddish-brown and enduring for centuries.

Land of two kings

Home of Phung Hung, Ngo Quyen and Giang Van Minh, the envoy who would not bow to the Ming court.

Fishbone lanes

Five hamlets linked by brick lanes that branch out like a fishbone, leading into every neighbourhood and every home.

Century-old houses

Wooden houses of three or five bays with brick courtyards and laterite walls. The oldest, Mr Nguyen Van Hung’s house, was built in 1649.

The journey

A slow day in Duong Lam

No packed schedule needed. This is the rhythm of a day that explains why so many people come back.

  1. Early morning

    Walk the lanes while the village wakes

    Mist still rests on the tiled roofs and kitchen smoke drifts over the laterite walls. The lanes are at their quietest and the light at its softest.

  2. Mid-morning

    Step into an old house

    Many families still live in wooden houses with brick courtyards and water tanks. Ask your host about the generations who grew up under that roof.

  3. Late morning

    The land of two kings

    Visit the Phung Hung temple and the Ngo Quyen mausoleum, then cross the fields at the edge of the village before the sun gets high.

  4. Noon

    A country lunch

    Garden greens, free-range chicken and a bowl of sticky rice soybean sauce – simple food you remember after a morning on foot.

  5. Afternoon

    Rest, coffee and gifts, then head home

    Take it slow with a coffee, pick up a few local gifts such as che lam or sticky rice soybean sauce, then say goodbye to Duong Lam.

Four seasons

Every season, a different Duong Lam

Spring

Festival time – flags in the lanes and families coming home.

Summer

Sunlight deepens the red of the laterite; shade under the old trees.

Autumn

Clear skies and a light breeze – the best season for walking and photos.

Winter

Mist, kitchen smoke and a warm sweet soup on an old veranda.

Visit gently

Keep the village a village

  • 1
    Ask before entering a house

    Old houses are homes. A greeting and a question are enough for most hosts to welcome you in.

  • 2
    Ask before photographing people

    Photograph the lanes freely; ask before taking pictures of people.

  • 3
    Dress modestly at temples

    Communal houses, pagodas and temples are places of worship for the whole village.

  • 4
    Leave only footprints

    Take your rubbish with you and never write on the laterite walls.

Getting there

How to reach Duong Lam

Motorbike or car
From central Hanoi take the Thang Long Avenue towards Son Tay town – about an hour.
Bus or coach
Both city buses and coaches from central Hanoi stop right at the village gate – from there it is a walk of a few hundred metres.
When to go
Weekday mornings are the quietest. Bring walking shoes and a hat.
